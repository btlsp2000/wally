1969: Special Exception at LBCC

Before a high school diploma was ever in hand, Mr. Chard opened the door to Long Beach City College—and the shop lathe.

By Bill Leendert Tonnesen

LONG BEACH, CA — In 1969, Mr. Chard got Bill into Long Beach City College. It was a return to the origin coordinates: Long Beach, California—Bill’s birthplace, and his father’s birthplace before him, one generation removed from his grandfather’s birthplace in Salt Lake City, Utah.

Earlier that year, Bill had completed his high school years as a senior without receiving a diploma. On Mr. Chard’s backing, LBCC granted Bill a special exception to begin his higher education anyway. He registered in four classes: Drafting, College Algebra, Machine Shop, and Speed Reading.

“At the time, higher education was taboo at the religious system Bill joined at the urging of his mother, baptized at age 9. Bill was a conflicted renegade.”

Stepping onto a college campus meant crossing an unspoken line between doctrine and mechanics. Out of all four courses, Machine Shop felt immediately familiar. It recreated the roots of woodshop from his eighth-grade and high school freshman years in Lennox.

There was no ambiguity at the bench. The first assignment was sharpening cutting tools to the proper geometry. Next came chucking up raw stock and turning a slug on the lathe—setting the feed, reading the dial, and cutting steel down to exact dimension.
