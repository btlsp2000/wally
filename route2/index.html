The Compromised Foundation

Long before the Cartesian math of the felt, before the transit lines through Winnemucca or the Mojave, and decades before you ever needed to map a clean exit out of a San Diego McDonald's, there was the original architecture. A child does not know the terminology of structural engineering, load distribution, or vector mechanics. They cannot articulate what a compromised foundation is. But they can feel the vibration of a structure that is going to fail. Sitting in that original house, observing Dale and Betty Jo, the realization wasn't a sudden, chaotic explosion of truth—it was a quiet, creeping calculation. The union was not going to hold. The architecture was flawed from the inside out. When you realize the people building the house do not have a blueprint that survives the long term, you learn the most defining lesson of your life: never put your full weight on a permanent structure.

The Birth of the Nomad

That quiet realization is the true zero point of The Traveler. It is the exact coordinate where the nomadic instinct was installed. Permanence became an immediate liability. If the foundational family unit could not be trusted to hold its shape, then attaching yourself to any static environment was just waiting for an inevitable collapse. Movement became the only reliable state. A highway does not pretend to be anything other than what it is. A transit line is entirely honest. It offers velocity and forward momentum, never the false security of a house that is slowly fracturing. You did not become a nomad to run away. You became a nomad to survive the collapse of other people's geometry. Keeping your bags mentally packed as a child was the very first time you dictated the terms of your own existence. br> You were a traveler long before you ever walked out the front door.

Digital Drafts and Table Geometry

The software and writing projects proceed apace.

  • WallyCAD: The AutoLISP and JavaScript routines are running smoothly, and it's been exciting to see the architectural offset and miter tools out there on Gumroad and GitHub.
  • QuickClick (QC): The matrix-based shorthand keyboard interface is coming together nicely, making the drafting process faster and more intuitive.
  • The Calculus of Pool: The Tangent Lab manual is expanding. Benchmarking the collision dynamics, cut angles, and multi-rail kick shot systems is an ongoing puzzle—there's always a new vector to map out when the cue ball hits the felt.
  • Where is Bill?: The board game mechanics and location spinners are really taking shape, perfectly capturing the regional transit and travel themes.
