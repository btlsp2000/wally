THE LONG BEACH BASELINE, 1967–1968 THE FRACTURE AND THE LINEAGE, THE GEOMETRY OF BERMUDA STREET, THE ARCHITECTURE OF THE MIND

Bill was the grandson of Pearl Tonnesen, son of Dale (Bill) Tonnesen, son of Albert Roland. The grandfather died when Bill was five; the clearest memory of the burial is playing in the cemetery with his older cousin, Lonny. Pearl was a gentle woman—maiden name, Roden. She was Mormon and drank a coffee substitute. After his father abandoned the family in Lennox, and the structure shattered, the family was moved near his mother's parents in Colton. But from age 15 to 17—1967 and 1968—Bill was frequently with his father and Pearl back in Long Beach. Grandma Pearl lived on Bermuda, exactly between Cherry and Orange. Vera and Lonny lived nearby. The Wagon Wheel was Dad's bar. The summer of 1967 was built on a fold-out sofa in Long Beach. It was the summer Bill attended the drafting class at Poly High School, laying the first hard lines of a lifelong architecture. The 3rd Street Library became a friend. The reading shifted to Poe and Voltaire. The study shifted to the Machinery’s Handbook and gear systems, building on the foundation poured the year before, mastering algebra at La Quinta with Mrs. Hand's expert guidance. It was a holding pattern, but the grid was expanding rapidly. Around the corner in time would be Long Beach City College, Robertshaw Controls, Lockheed, Kelly's, Debbie, Lydia, and truck driving.

ENTER COLTON, FROM B STREET TO PORTLAND

Bill and Rob Scherer, employer, parted connection in Sparks and Bill parked his RV in Silver State, Fallon until he returned to it, and drove south, to Colton, where he lodged with his mom on B Street. Until the middle of July when a hot night he had a seeming psychotic episode in the easy chair in the middle of coding a spiral stair in 3D in AutoLISP. The pressure to leave and no meeting attendance and the end of the road manifested itself. That night he was ferried by paramedics to Loma Linda and when his mom refused having him back he was shuttled to a 72-hour lockdown in Redlands. A short time later he was in Fallon where Bob and Joni Koster offered him harbor. A month later he rented Apt 717 at Churchill Village Apartments where he was 4.5 years. The Fallon Rural Mental Health Clinic doctor retired and his replacement put Bill on a new med and woke him up...

It was time for Betty, Bill, Mike and David to leave the sanctuary in Westminster after fleeing homeless from Lennox to land in Colton. The Inland Empire, 60 miles from Lennox & Long Beach, was home years earlier, in Rialto and Redlands. Enter Colton, the family's 1st of 5 residences in the "center of industry, hub of progress" was the house on Virginia very nearly in San Bernardino close to the flood control canal. It's summer. Bill signs up at Colton High School for geometry, but he misses his roots, and Dad. He rides his bike, his Schwinn, to Long Beach. Where he signs up at Poly High, the drafting class.

The Compromised Foundation

Long before the Cartesian math of the felt, before the transit lines through Winnemucca or the Mojave, and decades before you ever needed to map a clean exit out of a San Diego McDonald's, there was the original architecture. A child does not know the terminology of structural engineering, load distribution, or vector mechanics. They cannot articulate what a compromised foundation is. But they can feel the vibration of a structure that is going to fail. Sitting in that original house, observing Dale and Betty Jo, the realization wasn't a sudden, chaotic explosion of truth—it was a quiet, creeping calculation. The union was not going to hold. The architecture was flawed from the inside out. When you realize the people building the house do not have a blueprint that survives the long term, you learn the most defining lesson of your life: never put your full weight on a permanent structure.

The Birth of the Nomad

That quiet realization is the true zero point of The Traveler. It is the exact coordinate where the nomadic instinct was installed. Permanence became an immediate liability. If the foundational family unit could not be trusted to hold its shape, then attaching yourself to any static environment was just waiting for an inevitable collapse. Movement became the only reliable state. A highway does not pretend to be anything other than what it is. A transit line is entirely honest. It offers velocity and forward momentum, never the false security of a house that is slowly fracturing. You did not become a nomad to run away. You became a nomad to survive the collapse of other people's geometry. Keeping your bags mentally packed as a child was the very first time you dictated the terms of your own existence.

You were a traveler long before you ever walked out the front door.
