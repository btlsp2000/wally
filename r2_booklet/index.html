FAMILIES OF DISCORD

“From Grandma Pearl’s to his own family’s and others, Bill has sat and lived and dined and slept in many and varied spaces, assembling and disassembling and negotiating the long and constantly changing passage...”

A life mapped across the West reads like a structural field log—columns erected, bearing plates bolted down, rooms occupied for a season, and unbolted back into the transit stream when the ground shifts beneath the slab. The geometry of a household is governed less by the walls holding it up than by the unseen tensions running through the people sitting around its table.

The baseline coordinate sits in Long Beach (1967–1968) at Grandma Pearl’s house on Bermuda Street between Cherry and Orange—walking to the 3rd Street Library, studying Machinery’s Handbook, and drafting at Poly High without institutional negotiation. From Firmona Avenue and Vista Garden (“Cockroach Heaven”) to thirteen years in San Diego North County raising Wesley, Malcolm, and Justin, each address carried its own floor plan of discord and survival.

Even at family gatherings, selective compliance held the frame together—like Betty Jo pulling a full Thanksgiving turkey out of the oven in Colton while Lydia sat across the table quietly aghast, waiting to whisper her protest in private.
