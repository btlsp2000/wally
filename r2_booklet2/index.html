Firmona Avenue, 1966

The Master Blueprint: How One Household Fracture Echoed Across Sixty Years
Every name and city on the "DOING / DID ME WRONG" ledger is an echo of a single structural collapse in Lennox, California, when two opposite exits left a fifteen-year-old holding the mortar.

Before 1966, the house on Firmona Avenue in Lennox had the clean symmetry of an uncrossed blueprint. Three brothers—me at fifteen, Mike at thirteen, and little David at four—shared a baseline under one roof before the geometry broke.

When Dale walked out that year, he took the open road with him, leaving an empty chair and an unpaid ledger behind. A Korean War Air Force veteran and a truck driver who knew how to cover miles, he did the one thing a driver knows how to do when pressure hits its limit: he pulled out onto the highway and kept moving.

Betty Jo didn't take the road. Left alone on Firmona Avenue with three boys and a sudden void where a marriage used to be, she reached for the nearest airtight structure she could find—the Kingdom Hall. What began as a search for footing quickly hardened into total institutional alignment, culminating in her public declaration among the Anointed at a Memorial service while the living room at home remained divided.

Dropped into the gap between an absent father and an institution-driven mother at fifteen, I took on the job nobody assigned out loud: I became the mortar. Stepping in to stabilize broken ground—offering unscripted loyalty to people and systems running on rigid or transactional scripts—became the master template for the next six decades.

Putting ME as the final line on the clipboard ledger wasn't self-pity; it was a structural audit. None of the names or cities on that page could run an angle without my participation. Retiring the role of the Firmona mortar is where 1966 finally stops dictating the route ahead.

Structural Ledger Audit // 1966 – 2026

1. The Institutional Wall (Mom → Mike)

Betty Jo traded a fractured household for doctrinal certainty. Sixty years later, Mike—who shared that Firmona bedroom—repeated the exact pattern at the end in Oregon, choosing institutional alignment over blood.

2. The One-Way Siphon (Sandra & Iris)

Carrying weight that wasn't mine left an open handle for anyone fluent in crisis or helpfulness—from coworker PayPal requests over years to Medicare commission hooks and wrong-zip-code PDFs over weeks.

3. Civic & Transient Traps (Portland → Vegas)

The same reflex applied to geography: enduring eleven years in a decaying basement unit at The Metropolitan out of sheer stamina, then landing under sudden medical friction in Downtown Las Vegas.

4. Closing the Account (The Mark — Me)

Every ounce of energy spent auditing what past people or cities owed was fuel taken off my own board. Naming myself as the final mark ends the con immediately.
